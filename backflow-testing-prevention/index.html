Why Backflow Prevention Matters

A backflow preventer stops contaminated water, from an irrigation system, a commercial process, or a cross-connection, from siphoning backward into the clean municipal water supply. Most jurisdictions require annual testing to confirm the device is still functioning correctly.

In Cary, this comes up most often for homes and HOA-managed communities with irrigation systems, where the local water utility typically requires proof of annual testing to keep the connection active.

Missing a testing deadline can mean the water utility restricts or flags the connection until it's resolved, which is a bigger hassle than the test itself. Scheduling it before the deadline rather than after avoids that entirely.

Common Reason For This Call

"I Need My Irrigation Backflow Tested"

This is the single most common reason this page gets a call: an HOA or the water utility requires annual proof of testing to keep an irrigation connection active, and the deadline is either coming up or has already passed.

Backflow testing follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted by the North Carolina State Building Code, along with the local water utility's cross-connection control requirements.

FAQs

Common Questions

How often does backflow testing need to happen?

Annually in most cases, though your specific utility or HOA requirement may vary.

What happens if my device fails the test?

We repair or replace it, then retest before submitting certification, so it's one visit in most cases rather than two.

Do you install new backflow preventers for new irrigation systems?

Yes, including sizing and installing the correct device type for the system.

Do commercial properties have different requirements?

Often yes, depending on the type of business and cross-connection risk involved. We confirm the applicable requirement before testing.

Do you provide the test report for my records?

Yes, you receive a copy of the certified test results for your own records in addition to what's submitted to the utility or HOA.

Can testing be done without shutting off water to the whole house?

In most residential setups, yes, since the backflow device is isolated to the irrigation or specific line it protects.

What happens during the actual test itself?

A gauge is connected to the device to measure pressure differentials across its check valves, confirming water can't flow backward through it.

Do you handle testing for well water systems too?

Backflow requirements are typically tied to municipal water connections, but we can advise on cross-connection concerns for well systems as well.

Who do you submit the certification to?

Results go to whichever entity requires them, typically the local water utility or, for irrigation systems, the HOA managing the community.

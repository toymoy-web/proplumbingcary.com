Garbage Disposal Humming But Not Spinning: What To Do

This specific symptom has a specific, usually simple cause.

By the Cary Pro Plumbing team · September 14, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(919) 944-7281
Call Now

A garbage disposal that hums when switched on but doesn't actually spin is one of the more recognizable plumbing symptoms out there, and also one of the more fixable ones, provided the next steps are handled safely and in the right order.

What the Humming Actually Means

The hum is the disposal's motor trying to run. If it were a dead motor or a full electrical failure, there would typically be silence instead. A hum with no spinning almost always means the motor is receiving power but the impeller, the spinning component that grinds waste, is physically jammed by something caught inside.

Step One: Turn It Off and Unplug It

Before doing anything else, switch off the disposal and, if accessible, unplug it or shut off power at the breaker. Never reach into a disposal, even a switched-off one, with the unit still connected to power.

Step Two: Try the Reset Button

Most disposals have a small red reset button on the bottom of the unit. A jam sometimes trips an internal circuit breaker as a safety measure, and pressing reset after clearing an obvious obstruction can restore power without needing any further repair.

Step Three: Use an Allen Wrench, Not Your Hand

Many disposals have a hex-shaped opening at the bottom center that accepts an Allen wrench, letting you manually rotate the impeller back and forth to work a jam loose from outside the unit, without ever putting a hand near the blades. If the disposal came with this wrench, it's usually stored nearby; a generic quarter-inch hex wrench often works too.

What Usually Causes the Jam

Fibrous foods like celery or corn husks, bones, fruit pits, and utensils that accidentally fall in are the most common culprits. Ice cubes run through periodically can help knock loose minor buildup, though they won't clear an actual jam caused by a solid object.

When Humming Continues After Clearing an Obvious Jam

If the unit still hums with no spin after trying the reset button and manually rotating the impeller, the issue may be a failed motor bearing or a more serious internal problem that isn't a simple jam. At that point, professional garbage disposal repair makes more sense than continuing to force it, since repeated attempts can sometimes make an underlying mechanical failure worse.

Closing Note

Patience with the safe troubleshooting steps saves both the cost of an unnecessary repair call and the risk of making a simple jam worse.

Final Thought

Most of these calls end with a cleared jam, not a replaced unit, provided the safe troubleshooting steps happen first.

A Quick Recap of the Safe Steps

Turn it off and unplug it, try the reset button, then manually rotate the impeller with an Allen wrench from outside the unit. If humming continues after all three steps, that's the point to call rather than continuing to try things.

Choosing a Replacement If It Comes to That

Horsepower, noise level, and feed type, continuous versus batch feed, are the main decisions when replacing a unit. A slightly higher horsepower model generally handles heavier household use with fewer future jams than the minimum-rated option.

What a Professional Repair Visit Actually Checks

Beyond clearing the immediate jam, a proper repair visit checks the electrical connection, the reset mechanism itself, and the condition of the motor and seals, since a unit that jams repeatedly sometimes has an underlying issue that a single manual clear doesn't address.

Is Replacement Ever the Better Call

A disposal that's jammed repeatedly despite careful use, or one that's already a decade or more old, may be more cost-effective to replace than to keep repairing, especially once a motor issue rather than a simple jam is the actual cause.

Preventing the Next Jam

Running cold water while the disposal operates, avoiding fibrous or starchy foods in large quantities, and not using the disposal as a general trash disposal all reduce how often this happens. None of these guarantee it never will, since even careful use occasionally results in the wrong thing going down at the wrong time.

Standards Installation Work Follows

Disposal installation and repair follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted by the North Carolina State Building Code for drain connection and the electrical disconnect requirements specific to a hardwired or plugged-in unit.

Before You Try Anything

Turn off and unplug the unit first, and never reach into the disposal chamber by hand even when it's switched off, since manual rotation should always be done from outside using the hex opening at the bottom, never by hand from the top.

Cost Factors For This Repair

Clearing a jam costs far less than diagnosing and repairing a failed motor, and replacing a unit entirely costs more still, which is part of why trying the safe DIY steps first is worth the few minutes it takes before assuming the worst.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to smell burning and still try the reset button?

A burning smell suggests a more serious electrical issue. Leave the unit off and call rather than continuing to troubleshoot it yourself.

Can I pour anything down the disposal to clear a jam?

Avoid chemical drain cleaners in a disposal specifically; they're generally more effective and appropriate for actual drain clogs than for a jammed disposal.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

Around 8 to 12 years under normal household use, though what goes down it and how often affects that range meaningfully.

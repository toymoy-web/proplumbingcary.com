How Often Should You Flush Your Water Heater in Cary?

The generic "once a year" advice isn't wrong, but it's not the whole story either.

By the Cary Pro Plumbing team · September 14, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(919) 944-7281
Call Now

Flushing a water heater clears sediment that settles at the bottom of the tank, or scale on a tankless unit's heat exchanger. Skipping it doesn't cause sudden failure, but it does shorten the unit's working life and gradually reduce its efficiency, which is easy to miss until the difference has become significant.

Why Sediment Builds Up At All

Minerals naturally present in the water supply settle out as the water heats, and over time they accumulate at the bottom of a tank or on the surface of a tankless heat exchanger. Cary's water hardness means this happens somewhat faster here than it would in a softer-water region, which is the actual reason local guidance leans toward annual flushing rather than a longer interval.

Tank (standard household use)AnnuallyPopping or rumbling noise, slower recovery
Tank (high hot water use)Every 6 to 9 monthsSame symptoms, appearing sooner
TanklessAnnuallyReduced hot water output, error codes
Tankless (hard water, no softener)Every 6 to 9 monthsFaster output decline than a softened-water home

Tank Units: Annual Is the Reasonable Default

For a standard tank water heater, an annual flush clears sediment before it builds up enough to insulate the heating element or start causing the popping and rumbling noise that signals a tank in trouble. A household with particularly high hot water use might benefit from flushing slightly more often.

Tankless Units: The Same Interval, For a Different Reason

A tankless water heater doesn't collect sediment the way a tank does, but scale builds directly on the heat exchanger surface, gradually reducing output until a flush clears it. The annual interval is similar, though a tankless unit will show reduced hot water output as its main symptom rather than noise.

Signs You've Gone Too Long Without One

Popping or rumbling from a tank unit, or noticeably reduced output from a tankless one, both mean sediment or scale has already built up enough to affect performance. A flush at that point still helps, though it's a bigger job than a flush done on schedule, since more buildup takes more time to clear.

Can You Flush It Yourself?

Some homeowners do flush a tank unit themselves with the right tools and a bit of caution around the drain valve and water temperature. A tankless flush typically requires a pump kit and isolation valves that aren't always already installed on the unit, which is part of why it's more commonly left to a professional visit.

Final Thought

A water heater rarely fails without warning; sediment and scale buildup give plenty of notice for anyone paying attention to the signs.

A Quick Recap

Annual flushing for both tank and tankless units, more frequent for particularly high hot water use, is the reasonable default in Cary's water given local hardness. Skipping it doesn't cause sudden failure, but it does shorten the unit's working life measurably over time.

Combining a Flush With Other Maintenance

A water heater flush is sometimes scheduled alongside other seasonal home maintenance, like an HVAC check, since both benefit from being on a predictable annual cycle rather than an as-needed basis that's easy to let slide.

What If You've Never Flushed It At All

A unit that's gone years without any flush isn't necessarily beyond help, though the first flush on a long-neglected unit sometimes takes longer and occasionally reveals sediment buildup severe enough that a technician recommends replacement rather than continued maintenance, depending on what's found.

Setting a Reminder That Actually Works

Tying the flush to an already-memorable annual event, like a birthday month or the start of a season, works better in practice than relying on a generic maintenance app reminder that's easy to dismiss or forget entirely.

What a Professional Flush Adds Beyond the Basic Process

Beyond clearing sediment or scale, a professional visit typically includes checking the anode rod on a tank unit, inspecting the flow sensor and venting on a tankless unit, and confirming the whole system is functioning correctly, not just flushed. That additional inspection is often what catches a developing issue before it becomes a failure.

What Happens During An Actual Flush

For a tank unit, the water heater is shut off, the cold water supply closed, and the tank drained through its valve until the water runs clear, flushing out settled sediment. A tankless flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit using a pump and hoses connected to isolation valves, dissolving mineral scale from the heat exchanger surface.

Installation Standards That Apply Either Way

Water heater maintenance and any related repair work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted by the North Carolina State Building Code, regardless of unit type. This matters most when a flush reveals an issue that goes beyond routine maintenance, since any resulting repair still has to meet the same code requirements as a new installation.

Cost Of Flushing Versus Cost Of Skipping It

A routine flush costs relatively little compared to the cost of a water heater failing years earlier than its rated lifespan, or the cost of a repair caused by sediment buildup that a simple flush would have prevented entirely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does flushing void my water heater warranty if I do it myself?

Not typically, though the warranty usually requires proof that maintenance happened at all, which a self-service flush makes harder to document than a professional visit.

Will a water softener reduce how often I need to flush?

Yes, softened water significantly slows mineral buildup, which can extend the interval between flushes. See our water softener page for details.

Is a flush the same as a full inspection?

Not necessarily on its own, though a professional visit usually combines both, checking overall unit condition while flushing sediment or scale.

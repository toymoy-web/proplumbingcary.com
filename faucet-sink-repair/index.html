What Usually Causes A Dripping Faucet

A dripping faucet almost always comes down to a worn cartridge, O-ring, or washer inside the fixture, parts that wear out from years of use regardless of the faucet's age or brand.

Sink connection issues underneath, like a loose P-trap or a failing supply line fitting, are just as common a call, especially in homes where a previous DIY installation wasn't fully tightened or sealed.

A drip that seems minor adds up in wasted water over months, and the same worn cartridge that causes a drip today is often what fails into an actual leak later, which is why addressing it early tends to be the cheaper path.

Signs To Watch For

Signs You Need Faucet Or Sink Repair

Persistent Drip

A drip that continues even with the handle fully off points to a worn internal cartridge or washer.

Low Or Uneven Pressure

Weak flow from one faucet only usually means a clogged aerator or a failing cartridge, not a whole-house issue.

Water Under The Sink

Moisture in the cabinet below usually traces to a loose P-trap connection or a supply line fitting.

Handle Loose Or Hard To Turn

A stiff or wobbly handle often means internal wear that will eventually lead to a leak if left alone.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my faucet still drip after I replaced the washer myself?

A drip that continues after a washer swap usually means the cartridge itself, not just the washer, needs replacing.

Is a small drip worth fixing right away?

It's worth addressing before it worsens, both for the water waste and because the same wear often leads to a bigger leak later.

Can you match my existing faucet finish?

For a full replacement, yes, in most common finishes. For a repair, we work with the existing fixture using compatible parts.

What if the leak is actually the garbage disposal, not the faucet?

See our garbage disposal page. We check both while we're under the sink either way.

Do you repair pull-down or touchless faucets?

Yes, including the specific cartridge and sensor components those styles use, which differ from a standard two-handle faucet.

What if the sink itself is cracked, not just the faucet?

We can also handle sink replacement if the basin itself is damaged rather than just the fixture above it.

Is it normal for a new faucet to still drip slightly at first?

A brand-new faucet shouldn't drip at all. If it does, it's usually a connection that needs tightening rather than a fixture defect.

Do you handle outdoor spigot or hose bib repair too?

Yes, outdoor faucets and hose bibs are repaired using the same diagnostic approach as indoor fixtures, including freeze-related damage.

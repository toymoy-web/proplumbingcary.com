Apex Neighborhoods

Apex Neighborhoods We Cover

Apex splits fairly cleanly into two eras: the Salem Street Historic District, where homes date back to the town's 1900s railroad-depot roots, and newer growth areas like Hearthstone and Kelly Creek, built out over the last two decades. Providence sits in between, with a mix of both.

That split matters for plumbing more than the town's small size might suggest. Older homes near downtown are far more likely to need leak detection or a water heater nearing the end of its life, while newer builds in Hearthstone and Kelly Creek see more first-time fixture and installation work.

Apex is just west of Cary, close enough that our daily routes cover it without a separate dispatch, which is part of why coverage here looks the same as coverage in Cary itself: same technicians, same daily scheduling, same upfront pricing model regardless of which side of the town line a call comes from.

Local Context

Why Apex Homes Call Us

Historic Downtown Core

The salem street historic district has some of the oldest supply lines in town, making leak detection a regular call.

Newer Growth Areas

Hearthstone and Kelly Creek see more builder-grade fixture issues than aging-pipe problems.

Apex Union Depot

Apex's identity as a rail town, marked by the Apex Union Depot, an 1867 train depot that marks the highest point on the old Chatham Railroad and gives the town its name, is a reminder of just how much of the older housing stock predates modern plumbing code.

Local Utility

Apex runs Apex's own Water Resources Department, separate from Cary's utility, which is worth knowing if a water-quality or billing question comes up alongside a plumbing one.

24/7 Dispatch

Emergency calls from Apex get the same around-the-clock dispatch as every other stop on our routes, day or night.

Apex was incorporated in the 1870s, with its downtown designated a historic district in 1994, and that history shows up on the ground in a way most residents never think about until a plumbing issue forces the question: was this pipe run installed to a code from decades ago, or to the code in effect for a home built last year. We check the actual installation rather than assuming based on the neighborhood's general reputation, since a renovated older home can have newer plumbing than its exterior suggests, and the reverse is also true.

Emergency Prep

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

1

Shut Off The Main Valve

If it's safe to reach, shutting off the main water valve stops the source before anything else.

2

Kill Power To Wet Areas

If water is near outlets or a panel and it's reachable without stepping into water, shut off power to that area.

3

Clear The Immediate Area

Move what you can safely reach out of the way of spreading water before help arrives.

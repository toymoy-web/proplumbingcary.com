Fast Growth

One Of NC's Fastest-Growing Towns

Holly Springs was incorporated in 1877 and now one of the fastest-growing towns in North Carolina. Most of what exists today, including Sunset Ridge, 12 Oaks, and Bass Lake Farms, was built in just the last two decades, which shapes almost every plumbing call we get here.

That much new construction in a short window means the calls skew heavily toward builder-grade fixture issues and first-time installations rather than the aging-pipe problems common in older Wake County towns.

Holly Springs is south of Cary, part of the same fast-growing southwest Wake County corridor as Apex, and that pace of growth is part of why. A town that was still mostly rural within living memory now has enough rooftops that plumbing calls here look more like a newer Cary subdivision than an older Wake County town, regardless of the town's own long incorporation history. Emergency dispatch covers Holly Springs around the clock, the same as every other stop on our routes.

Downtown Holly Springs

The town's small historic core near Bass Lake Park and the Leslie-Alford-Mims House, a registered historic landmark.

The contrast between Bass Lake Park and Leslie-Alford-Mims House and the newer subdivisions around it is a useful shorthand for the town as a whole: a small historic core surrounded by nearly two decades of continuous new construction. Bass Lake Park and the surrounding older streets are the exception rather than the rule here, which is why aging-pipe calls are the minority of what we see in Holly Springs compared to almost every other town on our routes.

In Holly Springs' single 27540 ZIP code, fixture repair and first-time water heater installation are the two most common calls, reflecting how much of the town has been built in just the last two decades. Water damage restoration calls follow the same newer-construction pattern, usually tied to a fresh appliance or connection failure rather than an aging line. Because the whole town shares one ZIP code, pricing and scheduling don't vary by neighborhood the way they sometimes do in towns split across several ZIP codes.

Sump pump and crawlspace-related calls also show up more in Holly Springs' newer subdivisions than older Wake County towns, simply because so much of the housing here was built during a period of rapid grading and development. See our sump pump installation and repair page if a newer home has had water intrusion issues in a crawlspace during heavy rain. All work here follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted by the North Carolina State Building Code.

Emergency Prep

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

1

Shut Off The Main Valve

If it's safe to reach, shutting off the main water valve stops the source before anything else.

2

Kill Power To Wet Areas

If water is near outlets or a panel and it's reachable without stepping into water, shut off power to that area.

3

Clear The Immediate Area

Move what you can safely reach out of the way of spreading water before help arrives.

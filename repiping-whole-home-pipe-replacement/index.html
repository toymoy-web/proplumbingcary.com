When One-Off Repairs Stop Making Sense

A single pipe repair fixes a single failure point. When a home has had two or three unrelated leaks within a couple of years, the pipe material itself, not any one section of it, is usually the actual problem.

This comes up most in Cary's older sections built before PEX became standard, where original copper or, in some cases, polybutylene supply lines are reaching the end of their expected service life all at roughly the same time, which is why failures cluster instead of spreading out evenly.

A phased repipe, room by room, keeps the home livable during the work and spreads the cost over time, which is often the more practical path for an occupied home compared to a single all-at-once project.

Signs To Watch For

Signs A Repipe Is Worth Considering

Repeated Unrelated Leaks

Multiple leaks in different rooms within a short window points to the pipe material, not bad luck.

Discolored Water Throughout

Rust-colored water from multiple fixtures, not just one, suggests corrosion inside the supply lines themselves.

Low Pressure Everywhere

Pressure loss at every fixture, not just one, often means mineral buildup narrowing pipes house-wide.

Polybutylene Pipe

Homes built with gray polybutylene pipe are past its known failure window regardless of visible symptoms yet.

Material Choice

PEX Or Copper

PEX

  • Lower material and labor cost
  • Flexible, fewer joints needed
  • Resistant to the corrosion that affects copper
  • Faster installation

Copper

  • Higher material cost
  • Longer proven track record
  • Preferred in some local code contexts
  • More rigid, more joints in tight spaces

Recognize This

"We've Had Three Different Leaks In Two Years"

This is the exact pattern that usually leads to a repiping conversation: not one dramatic failure, but a string of smaller ones in different parts of the house that all trace back to pipe age rather than anything specific to each individual spot.

Before We Arrive

What To Do Before The Assessment

List Past Leak Locations

Note where and when any previous leaks happened, even minor ones, to help map the pattern.

Clear Wall And Attic Access

Clear furniture or storage from areas near known plumbing walls where inspection access may be needed.

Plan For Water Shutoffs

Expect water to be off in sections during active work, and plan household activities around that if the repipe is phased.

Repiping follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted by the North Carolina State Building Code, including material and support spacing requirements for whichever pipe type is used.

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

It varies with home size and access, and can often be phased room by room to keep the home livable during the work.

Do you have to open every wall?

Access points are targeted where possible, though some wall opening is unavoidable for a full repipe. We patch as we go.

Is PEX as durable as copper?

Yes for residential supply lines, and it's less prone to the pinhole corrosion that affects aging copper in this water profile.

What if I just want one section repaired instead?

See our leak detection page if this is a single, isolated leak rather than a repeated pattern.

Will my water be off for the whole project?

Water is shut off only during active work on a given section, and we coordinate timing to minimize how long any part of the home is without water.

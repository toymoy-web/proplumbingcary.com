Why Septic Systems Need Regular Pumping

A septic tank separates solids from wastewater, and those solids accumulate over time. Without periodic pumping, solids eventually reach the outlet and start clogging the drain field, which is a far more expensive problem than pumping itself.

This applies to the parts of Cary and the surrounding Wake County area still on septic rather than municipal sewer, typically older or more rural-adjacent properties at the edges of the service area rather than the established in-town neighborhoods.

Skipping a scheduled pumping doesn't just risk a backup inside the home. Solids that reach the drain field can clog the soil itself, and a clogged drain field is a far more expensive repair than the tank pumping that would have prevented it, sometimes requiring a partial or full field replacement.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Septic Tank Needs Pumping

Slow Drains Throughout

Every drain in the house running slow, not just one, points to the septic system rather than an internal clog.

Sewage Odor Outside

A smell near the tank or drain field usually means it's reached capacity.

Standing Water Over The Tank

Soggy ground or standing water over the tank area is a sign it may be overfull.

Gurgling Toilets

Toilets gurgling after flushing, especially the one furthest from the tank, is an early warning sign.

Before We Arrive

How To Get Ready For Pumping

Mark The Tank If Known

If you know the tank's location, flag it so the crew doesn't need to search for it on arrival.

Keep Vehicles Off The Area

Avoid parking or driving over the tank or drain field, since the added weight can damage buried components.

Reduce Water Use Beforehand

Light water use in the hours before pumping makes the tank easier and faster to access and empty.

Septic service follows North Carolina's on-site wastewater regulations administered through the local county health department, which sets tank size and pumping interval guidance.

FAQs

Common Questions

How often should a septic tank be pumped?

Every 3 to 5 years is a common guideline, though household size and tank capacity both affect the right interval for a specific home.

Can you find my septic tank if I don't know where it is?

Yes, locating the tank is part of the service if it isn't already marked or documented.

Is a backup an emergency?

An active sewage backup into the home is treated as an emergency call. See our drain cleaning page if the backup turns out to be an internal clog rather than the tank itself.

Do you also repair drain fields?

We inspect and diagnose drain field issues during service and can advise on next steps if the field itself is the problem.

What can damage a septic system besides not pumping it?

Flushing non-flushable items, excess grease down the kitchen drain, and heavy vehicle traffic over the drain field all shorten a system's life.

Do you handle septic systems for larger properties or rural lots?

Yes, including larger tanks and systems on properties at the edge of Cary's service area.

Can heavy rain affect my septic system?

Yes, saturated ground around the drain field can slow drainage temporarily, which sometimes mimics the symptoms of a tank needing pumping.

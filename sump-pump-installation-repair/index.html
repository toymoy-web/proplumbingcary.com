Why Sump Pumps Fail At The Worst Time

A sump pump usually fails for one of three reasons: the float switch has stuck, the pump motor has burned out, or the discharge line is clogged or frozen. All three look the same from the outside, which is a pump that isn't turning on despite rising water.

Homes with crawlspaces in lower-lying parts of Cary are the most common candidates for a first-time sump pump installation, particularly where grading sends water toward the foundation during heavy rain.

Testing the float switch and motor separately is what tells us which of these it actually is, rather than replacing the whole unit when a single part would have solved it.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention

Not Turning On

Water rising with no pump activity usually means a stuck float switch or a dead motor.

Running Constantly

A pump that never shuts off may have a stuck switch in the other direction, wearing the motor out early.

Unusual Noise

Grinding or rattling often means debris in the impeller or a failing motor bearing.

No Pump Installed Yet

A crawlspace or low area that takes on water during heavy rain is a candidate for a first installation.

Common Situation

"The Water's Rising And The Pump Isn't Running"

This is treated as an urgent call for good reason: once a sump pump stops working during active rain, water can rise quickly in a crawlspace or basement. Getting a technician out fast to restore pumping, even temporarily, matters more here than in most non-emergency plumbing situations.

Sump pump installation follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted by the North Carolina State Building Code for discharge routing and backflow prevention on the discharge line.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need a sump pump if I don't currently have water issues?

If the crawlspace has ever come close to standing water during heavy rain, installing one proactively is usually cheaper than dealing with damage later.

How long does a sump pump last?

Typically 7 to 10 years under normal use, though a pump that runs constantly wears out faster.

Is a battery backup worth it?

For areas prone to flooding during storms, which often also cause power outages, yes, since that's exactly when a standard pump can't run.

What should I do if my crawlspace is actively flooding right now?

Call immediately. If water has already reached flooring above the crawlspace, see our water damage restoration page as well.

If water has already gotten in before the pump was serviced. If so, our Water Damage Restoration page covers that directly. For the emergency drying phase if a crawlspace has flooded. That's a job for Water Mitigation instead.

If the water source affecting the crawlspace turns out to be a plumbing leak, not just rain. In that case, take a look at our Leak Detection page.

Why Toilets Develop The Same Few Problems

A running toilet almost always comes down to a worn flapper or fill valve, both inexpensive parts. A toilet that rocks at the base usually means the wax ring seal has failed or the flange underneath is damaged, which is a different repair entirely and one that risks a floor leak if left alone.

In older Cary homes, a cracked or damaged flange is more common simply due to the age of the original installation, while a running toilet is just as likely in a newer Amberly or Copperleaf home once the internal parts wear out after years of normal use.

A running toilet is mostly a nuisance and a water bill problem, while a rocking one is a developing leak that can damage subflooring if it goes unaddressed. Telling the two apart up front is what decides whether a visit is routine or worth scheduling sooner.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Of A Toilet Problem

Running Constantly

Water running or refilling on its own points to a worn flapper or fill valve.

Rocking At The Base

Any movement when you sit down means the seal or flange needs attention before it leaks onto the floor.

Water Around The Base

Visible water at the floor is often a failed wax ring seal, not the tank itself.

Weak Flush

A flush that doesn't fully clear the bowl can mean a partial clog or a worn flush mechanism.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my toilet keep running?

Almost always a worn flapper or fill valve, both quick, inexpensive fixes in most cases.

Is a rocking toilet urgent?

It's worth addressing soon rather than waiting, since a failed seal underneath can leak onto the subfloor and cause damage over time.

Can you match my existing toilet's bolt pattern?

Standard rough-in spacing fits nearly all installations, and we confirm the specific measurement before quoting a replacement.

Do you handle water-efficient or comfort-height models?

Yes, we stock and install standard, comfort-height, and water-efficient toilets.

Do you remove the old toilet when installing a new one?

Yes, haul-away of the old fixture is included as part of a replacement.

Can a running toilet actually raise my water bill noticeably?

Yes, a continuously running toilet can waste a significant amount of water over a billing cycle, often more than homeowners expect.

Can a toilet be too old to find replacement parts for?

Very old models sometimes need a full replacement rather than parts, since exact internal components may no longer be manufactured.

Do newer toilets really use less water per flush?

Yes, water-efficient models use significantly less per flush than older units, which adds up over time on the water bill.

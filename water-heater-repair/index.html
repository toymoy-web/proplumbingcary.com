What's Actually Wrong When The Hot Water Stops

A failed heating element, a bad thermostat, and sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank all produce the same symptom: cold showers. The fix is completely different for each one, which is why a real diagnosis matters more than a guess based on the unit's age alone.

Cary's water carries enough mineral content that sediment buildup shortens tank life noticeably faster than it would in an area with softer water, especially in older Lochmere and MacGregor Downs homes still running their original tank. That buildup is also the most common cause of the popping or rumbling noise homeowners describe when they call.

Because those three failure points look similar from the outside, a technician has to actually test the element and thermostat rather than assume based on symptoms alone. That's the difference between paying for a part that fixes the problem and paying for a part that turns out not to have been the issue at all.

For tankless units specifically, the equivalent issue is scale buildup on the heat exchanger rather than sediment in a tank, which is why a tankless unit is usually flushed and descaled rather than repaired the same way a tank unit would be.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Water Heater Needs Attention

No Hot Water

Cold showers or a tank that runs out faster than it used to, both point to a heating element or thermostat issue.

Rusty Water

Discolored hot water, especially only from hot taps, usually means internal tank corrosion.

Popping Noises

Rumbling or popping as the unit heats is sediment buildup hitting the heating element.

Water Around The Base

Any moisture at the bottom of the tank means the tank itself may be failing, not just a part inside it.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is it worth repairing an older water heater?

It depends on what failed. See our replacement page for when a new unit makes more sense than another repair.

Do you repair tankless water heaters too?

Yes, tankless units are diagnosed and repaired the same day in most cases, including descaling for hard-water buildup.

Why does my water heater make a popping noise?

That's almost always sediment at the bottom of the tank. It's fixable now but is also a sign the tank is aging.

Can hard water really shorten a water heater's life?

Yes. Mineral buildup on the heating element and at the bottom of the tank is one of the most common reasons tanks in this area fail earlier than their rated lifespan.

How do I know if the problem is the thermostat or the heating element?

A technician tests each component individually rather than guessing, since both produce similar symptoms but need different parts.

Is it normal for a water heater to take longer to recover between uses as it ages?

Yes, slower recovery is one of the most common early signs of sediment buildup or a failing heating element, often before a full failure happens.
